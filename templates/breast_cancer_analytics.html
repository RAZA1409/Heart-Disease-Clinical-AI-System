📉 Clinical Diagnostics & Validation Curves

Cytology Risk Stratification

Distribution of evaluated biopsy cohorts across Low, Moderate, and High clinical risk tiers

Clinical Interpretation: Risk stratifications guide biopsy pathology workflow: Low (<30%) indicates benign morphology with routine mammography surveillance; Moderate (30%–70%) triggers re-biopsy / immunohistochemistry; High (≥70%) indicates urgent oncological staging.

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve

Diagnostic trade-off between True Positive Rate and False Positive Rate (AUC: 0.9954)

Clinical Interpretation: An exceptional ROC-AUC of 0.9954 confirms that the RBF kernel maps fine needle aspirate (FNA) nuclear boundaries into a hyperplane where malignant and benign tissue are almost completely linearly separable.

Literature-Reported Diagnostic Markers (WDBC)

Reference clinical significance from Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer literature (Street et al., 1993)

Literature Context: These markers reflect published morphological rankings from the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer study. Because the production classifier is a non-linear RBF Support Vector Machine, it does not generate native linear feature weights; these rankings are literature-based reference markers and not model-derived feature importance values.

Clinical Diagnostic Evaluation Profile

Balanced sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F1 performance across held-out test cohort

Clinical Interpretation: On the held-out test split, the model demonstrated 98.61% specificity (71 of 72 benign cases correctly identified, 1 false positive) and 97.62% sensitivity (41 of 42 malignant cases correctly identified, 1 false negative).
